About

I am an applied mathematician and quantitative researcher based in Homer Glen, Illinois. I successfully defended my Ph.D. dissertation in Applied Mathematics at Arizona State University on November 6, 2026. The dissertation was subsequently approved, with formal degree conferral scheduled for December 14, 2026. My work combines causal inference, statistical modeling, optimization, and economic simulation to answer questions in public policy, insurance, and risk management.

In my disaster-resilience research, I developed a 437-equation regional economic model of Louisiana and evaluated insurer cash incentives and annual premium relief for residential wind retrofits. The analysis compares household participation, insurer net present value, and the broader economic consequences of different incentive designs.

My other research includes designing an optimization-based matching procedure for 390 law-enforcement agency pairs across 31 states, estimating the effects of Ring Neighbors activation using stacked difference-in-differences, and analyzing approximately 110 million offense records in work on natural hazards and crime. I have published first-author research in the American Journal of Criminal Justice and coauthored two technical reports for Department of Homeland Security-funded research.

I am interested in opportunities in quantitative research, insurance and risk analytics, applied data science, and operations research. I value transparent assumptions, careful evaluation of uncertainty, and communicating technical findings clearly to decision-makers.
