Digital community policing and reported offenses

Causal inference · Stacked difference-in-differences · Event studies

Problem. Assess changes in reported offenses following activation of verified Ring Neighbors accounts by law enforcement agencies.

Approach and contribution. Developed an optimization-based matching design with 390 agency pairs across 31 states, and analyzed monthly offenses using stacked difference-in-differences, event studies and placebo analyses.

Output and scope. The observational study concerns agency-account activation, not household camera ownership or use. Its causal interpretation depends on identifying assumptions.

Residential wind retrofits and insurer incentives

Computable general equilibrium · Financial modeling · Scenario analysis · GAMSPy/CONOPT4

Problem. Evaluate additional public grants and insurer-funded incentives for residential wind retrofits in Louisiana.

Approach and contribution. Developed a 437-equation static regional economic model and evaluated 248,292 incentive combinations per targeting strategy, comparing participation, insurer financial feasibility and economy-wide effects.

Output and scope. Scenario results depend on model assumptions. This is not a storm simulation, dynamic forecast or commercial catastrophe model. Supported by the AXA Research Fund–Swiss Re Institute Joint Risk Resilience Partnership.

Natural hazards and crime

Administrative data · Statistical modeling · Spatiotemporal analysis

Problem. Investigate relationships between natural hazards and reported crime in the United States.

Approach and contribution. Integrated and analyzed approximately 110 million offense records and 216,000 hazard-loss records, aligning geographic identifiers and event dates.

Output and scope. First-author article published in the American Journal of Criminal Justice in 2025. The study found that changes in reported crime differed by hazard type and observation period; increases associated with some hazards over short windows did not persist over longer windows. An earlier CINA research report is also publicly available.

Cybercrime forecasting and resource allocation

Elastic net · Spatiotemporal regression · Forecasting

Problem. Study geographic and temporal patterns of cybercrime and their implications for public-safety resource allocation.

Approach and contribution. Applied feature selection and geographically and temporally weighted regression (GTWR) to examine state-level cybercrime severity and its socioeconomic and technological determinants; developed forecasts and communicated findings to research stakeholders.

Output and scope. Collaborative research supported by the DHS-funded CINA Center. Public project materials include backtesting against 2022 observations, forecasts of compromised records for 2024 by state and FBI field office, and illustrative personnel-allocation estimates. The companion project website was developed by co-researcher Stefano Chiaradonna.

Technical project · Public notebook

Constrained matching for crime research

Python · SciPy/HiGHS · Mixed-integer optimization

Problem. Identify comparable law enforcement agencies when evaluating an intervention using observational data.

Approach and contribution. Prepare agency-level panels, distinguish missing records from observed zeros, construct eligible pairs, and optimize one-to-one matching. Assess balance and retention before outcome modeling.

Output and scope. The public notebook selects 79 initial pairs from 1,630 synthetic candidates and documents how follow-up restrictions affect balance. It does not reproduce original study data or estimate treatment effects. The example is motivated by the digital community policing study.
